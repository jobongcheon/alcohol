30, 60, 90 and 180 days from a date

Many deadlines are written in days rather than months: a 30-day return window, net-60 invoice terms, a 90-day probation period or a 180-day warranty. This one calculator answers all of them — just change the number of days.

How to use the days from today calculator

  1. The answer for today appears automatically at the top.
  2. To use another start date or number of days, change them below.
  3. Tick “business days” to skip weekends.
  4. Press Calculate.

The formula

Date = start date + number of days (calendar days include weekends)

Business days skip Saturdays and Sundays only. Public holidays such as Christmas or New Year’s Day are not removed, so subtract any that fall inside your period.

Deadlines written into federal consumer law

Store return windows and job probation periods are company policy. These periods are set by federal law, and each one counts a little differently:

RulePeriodHow it is counted
Dispute a credit card billing error (Fair Credit Billing Act)60 daysFrom the date the statement with the error was sent to you; the dispute must be in writing
Credit card statement to payment due date (CARD Act)21 daysAt least 21 days must pass between the statement being mailed or delivered and the due date
Cancel a refinance or home equity loan (Truth in Lending)3 business daysAfter closing; Sundays and federal holidays do not count, Saturdays do
Dispute a debt after a collector's validation notice (FDCPA)30 daysFrom the day you receive the notice, in writing
Grace period on federal Direct Loans6 monthsWritten in months, not days, after you leave school or drop below half-time

Note that a "business day" is not the same in every law: for the rescission rule a Saturday counts.

Days are not the same as months

“30 days” and “1 month” only match in some months. From January 31, 30 days later is March 2, but one month later is usually the end of February. Contracts that say “months” follow calendar months; contracts that say “days” follow this calculator.

Does the start day count?

Here the start date is day 0, so the result is the day the period ends. Some rules count the first day as day 1, which makes the deadline one day earlier. When money or legal rights are involved, read the exact wording or ask the company.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming weekends are skipped — most consumer deadlines use calendar days.
  • Forgetting holidays when you count business days.
  • Mixing time zones for online deadlines that end at midnight in a different zone.

Before you rely on the number

  • For legal or official deadlines, check whether the first day counts and whether holidays are excluded.

Holidays that stretch a business-day count in early 2027

From Tuesday, December 1, 2026, 30 business days lands on Tuesday, January 12, 2027, skipping only weekends. If the other party also closes for federal holidays, add Christmas (Friday, December 25) and New Year's Day (Friday, January 1): the deadline moves to Thursday, January 14.

  • Within the 90-business-day window (April 6, 2027), add Martin Luther King Jr. Day (January 18) and Washington's Birthday (February 15), four holidays in all.
  • Within the 180-business-day window (August 10, 2027), add Memorial Day (May 31), Juneteenth observed on Friday, June 18 because the 19th is a Saturday, and Independence Day observed on Monday, July 5 because the 4th is a Sunday: seven holidays.

Banks and federal agencies follow this list; private companies choose their own. When a deadline lands on a weekend or holiday, many rules, including the federal tax deadline, roll it to the next business day; not every contract does.

Deadline questions people ask about counting days

What date is 90 days from today?
The box at the top always shows today’s answer for 30 days; type 90 in the number field and press Calculate.

Can I count backwards?
Yes. The result also lists the date the same number of days before your start date.

Is 30 days the same as 1 month?
Not exactly. Months have 28 to 31 days, so counting days gives a precise date.

Does today count?
No. Today is day 0, so tomorrow is day 1 and the result is day 30.
