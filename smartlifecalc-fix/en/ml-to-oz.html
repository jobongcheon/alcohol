Milliliters and fluid ounces: US vs UK

A fluid ounce measures volume, and there are two of them: the US fluid ounce (29.5735 ml) and the UK imperial fluid ounce (28.4131 ml). Most American labels and recipes use the US ounce.

The formula

US fl oz = ml ÷ 29.5735  |  ml = US fl oz × 29.5735

Worked examples

  • A 500 ml water bottle = 500 ÷ 29.5735 = 16.9 US fl oz.
  • An 8 fl oz cup of coffee = 8 × 29.5735 = 236.6 ml.
  • A 2-liter soda bottle = about 67.6 US fl oz.
  • A 120 ml baby bottle feed = about 4.06 fl oz.

Standard U.S. drink sizes converted to milliliters

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans define one standard drink as 12 fluid ounces of regular beer, 5 fluid ounces of wine or 1.5 fluid ounces of distilled spirits. Bottles, however, are sold in metric sizes, so the conversion decides how many servings you actually have.

Cups and spoons

  • 1 US cup = 8 fl oz = 236.6 ml (nutrition labels round it to 240 ml).
  • 1 tablespoon = 14.8 ml; 1 teaspoon = 4.9 ml.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing fluid ounces with weight ounces: 8 fl oz of honey weighs about 12 oz.
  • Using a UK recipe with US cups: check which country the recipe comes from.
  • Air-travel limits: the 3.4 oz rule is really 100 ml; the container size counts, not how full it is.

When the US and UK difference matters

For a single glass of water the difference between US and UK ounces is tiny, but it adds up in larger amounts. A 20-ounce British pint is about 95 ml more than a 16-ounce American pint. Recipes from Britain, Australia or older cookbooks may use imperial measures.

Medicine and baby formula deserve extra care: use the measuring cup, syringe or scoop that comes with the product rather than converting in your head, and follow the label or your doctor’s directions.

Quick reminders

  • TSA carry-on liquid limit: 3.4 oz (100 ml) per container.

The 64-ounce day in bottles and liters

The familiar advice to drink eight 8-ounce glasses adds up to 64 fl oz, or 1.89 liters. In the 16.9-ounce (500 ml) bottles sold in packs, that is 3.8 bottles. A 32-ounce refillable bottle (946 ml) needs two fills. A 1-liter bottle holds 33.8 ounces, so two of them is slightly more than the 64-ounce target.

The eight-glasses figure is a rule of thumb rather than an official standard. The Dietary Guidelines recommend water as the main beverage but set no glass count, and water from food, coffee and other drinks also counts toward intake. Needs rise with heat and exercise.

Going the other way, a European label that says 2 L a day means 67.6 ounces, about eight and a half 8-ounce glasses. For any amount in this range, the US-UK difference is about 4%: 64 US fl oz is 1,893 ml, while 64 imperial fl oz is 1,818 ml.
