Congratulations! Find your estimated due date, how far along you are today and when each trimester begins. All you need is the first day of your last menstrual period.

How your due date is estimated

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), not from conception, which for a typical 28-day cycle happens about two weeks later. A full-term pregnancy is estimated at 280 days, or 40 weeks, from the LMP. This is known as Naegele's rule, and it's the same starting point most clinics use before an ultrasound.

How to use the due date calculator

  1. Choose the first day of your last period.
  2. Enter your usual cycle length (28 days is typical).
  3. Press Calculate Due Date.

The formula

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days (+ cycle length − 28)

Worked example

If your last period started on January 1, 2026, adding 280 days gives an estimated due date of October 8, 2026. If your cycle is usually 32 days rather than 28, ovulation tends to happen later, so the estimate moves about 4 days later.

Worked calendar: a last period starting March 1, 2026

Counting 280 days forward from March 1, 2026 gives a due date of December 6, 2026. Every milestone follows from the same count, 7 days per week.

Why the date is only an estimate

Only about 1 in 20 babies arrive on the exact due date. Most are born between 37 and 42 weeks. An early ultrasound, especially in the first trimester, is the most accurate way to date a pregnancy, and your provider may adjust the due date based on it. If you conceived through IVF, the transfer date gives a very precise estimate.

Term definitions

  • Early term: 37 weeks 0 days to 38 weeks 6 days
  • Full term: 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days
  • Late term: 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days
  • Post-term: 42 weeks and beyond

Before you rely on the number

  • Start a prenatal vitamin with folic acid as early as possible.

When an ultrasound replaces the period-based date

Under American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists guidance, the LMP date stands unless an ultrasound differs from it by more than a set number of days, and the allowance grows as pregnancy advances.

Example: your LMP puts you at 8 weeks 2 days, and the scan measures 7 weeks 4 days. The gap is 5 days, so the LMP date stays. Had the scan read 7 weeks 3 days, the 6-day gap would move your due date 6 days later. Once set this way, the date is normally not changed again, even if a later scan suggests a bigger or smaller baby; IVF pregnancies are dated from the transfer instead.

Questions expectant parents ask most

I don't remember my last period. What now?
An ultrasound can date the pregnancy. Contact your healthcare provider.

Can I use this if my cycles are irregular?
It gives a rough estimate, but an ultrasound will be more reliable.

How accurate is a due date?
It is an estimate. An early ultrasound is the most accurate way to date a pregnancy.

Why count from my last period instead of conception?
Conception date is rarely known exactly, so doctors count from the last period, which adds about 2 weeks.
