How old are you exactly — to the day? This calculator shows your age in years, months and days, how many days you have lived, the day of the week you were born, and how long until your next birthday.

How exact age is counted

Your age in years changes on your birthday, but age in months and days needs a rule, because months have different lengths. This calculator counts complete years first, then complete months, then the remaining days — the same way most forms and official documents do.

How to use the age calculator

  1. Choose your date of birth.
  2. Leave the second date as today, or choose any other date.
  3. Press Calculate Age.

The formula

Age = years and months between the dates, then the remaining days

Worked example

Someone born on June 15, 1990 is, on November 14, 2026: 36 complete years (June 15, 2026 was the 36th birthday), plus 4 complete months (to October 15), plus 30 days (October 15 to November 14). Result: 36 years, 4 months, 30 days.

Where exact age matters

  • Benefits and retirement – Social Security full retirement age is 67 for people born in 1960 or later; you can claim from 62 with a reduced benefit.
  • Medicare – eligibility generally begins at 65.
  • School enrollment – many districts set a cut-off date for kindergarten age.
  • Insurance and licenses – driver's licenses, car insurance rates and some travel rules depend on exact age.

Leap-year birthdays

If you were born on February 29, your birthday falls on that date only every four years. For legal purposes, many U.S. states treat March 1 as the birthday in common years, while some other countries use February 28. The calculator follows the calendar strictly and shows both the years and the days.

Fun facts you can check

  • You reach 10,000 days old at about 27 years and 4 months.
  • 1 billion seconds is about 31.7 years.

Worth knowing

  • Use a future date to find how old you will be at an event.
  • People born on February 29 usually celebrate on February 28 or March 1 in non-leap years.

The month-end problem: August 31 to September 30

Imagine someone born on August 31, 2000. On September 30, 2026 they have clearly lived 26 complete years. But how many months since August 31? September has no 31st, so one tool reports 26 years, 1 month, 0 days (treating the 30th as the matching day) while another reports 26 years, 0 months, 30 days. Both are defensible. The same thing happens for anyone born on the 29th, 30th or 31st for part of every year.

This is why documents that depend on age almost always use whole years, and why total days lived is the one number every calculator agrees on. If you need a months figure for a form, check how that agency counts.

One rule catches people out: for Social Security, you are treated as reaching an age on the day before your birthday. Someone born on the 1st of a month is therefore considered to attain age 62 in the previous month, which can move their earliest eligible month of benefits earlier.

Things readers ask about counting their age

Does time zone matter?
For age in days, no. Age changes at midnight local time on your birthday.

Why do different calculators disagree by a day?
Some count the end date as a full day and some don't. This calculator does not count the start date, which is the usual convention.

How many days old am I?
The result shows total days lived — a fun number to celebrate, like your 10,000th day (about 27.4 years).

Why does age by year differ in some countries?
Some countries traditionally counted age differently, but international age is counted from birth as shown here.
