Calculating work hours without mistakes

Adding up time is harder than adding normal numbers because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. That's where most timesheet errors come from. This calculator handles minutes, overnight shifts and unpaid breaks, and shows the result both as hours and minutes and as a decimal for payroll.

How to use the hours calculator

  1. Choose “Hours between two times” or “Add or subtract time”.
  2. Pick the times with hours, minutes and AM/PM.
  3. Enter a break or an hourly rate if you like.
  4. Press Calculate.

The formula

Hours worked = end time − start time − break  |  decimal hours = minutes ÷ 60

Worked example

Clock in 8:45 AM, clock out 5:15 PM, with a 30-minute unpaid lunch. 8:45 to 5:15 is 8 hours 30 minutes; minus 30 minutes gives 8 hours, or 8.00 decimal hours. At $22 an hour that day is $176.

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, overtime is owed for hours over 40 in a single workweek. At $20 an hour: 40 × $20 = $800, plus 2.08 overtime hours × $30 = $62.50, for $862.50. The workweek counts, not the pay period: 45 hours one week and 35 the next is 5 overtime hours even though the two-week total is 80. And decimals come from minutes ÷ 60, so 42 hours 5 minutes is 42.08, not 42.05.

Overnight shifts

If you start at 10:00 PM and finish at 6:30 AM, the end time is "earlier" than the start time. The calculator treats it as the next day: the shift is 8 hours 30 minutes.

Overtime basics (U.S.)

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, most non-exempt employees earn at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Some states, such as California, also have daily overtime rules. Total your weekly hours here, then check your state's rules.

Before you rely on the number

  • Night shifts that pass midnight are handled automatically.

Rounding, daily overtime and a 2026 tax wrinkle

  • Rounding is legal within limits. Federal regulations allow rounding punches to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour, provided the practice does not consistently favor the employer. Under quarter-hour rounding, 8:07 becomes 8:00 but 8:08 becomes 8:15.
  • Some states count the day, not just the week. California requires 1.5× pay after 8 hours in a day and 2× after 12, so the Thursday shift above would carry 1 hour 45 minutes of overtime even in a short week. Alaska, Nevada and Colorado have their own daily rules with different triggers.
  • The overtime premium is deductible for 2025 through 2028. A 2025 federal law lets workers deduct the premium half of FLSA overtime, the extra $10 an hour in the example above ($20.83 for the week), up to $12,500 a year for a single filer or $25,000 on a joint return, with phase-outs at higher incomes.

Apply your state's daily rule on top of the 40-hour test and use whichever gives more pay.

Timesheet and overtime questions

Are breaks paid?
Under federal rules, short breaks (about 5–20 minutes) are usually paid; meal breaks of 30 minutes or more where you are fully relieved of duty usually are not.

How do I add up a whole week?
Calculate each day, convert to decimal hours, then add the decimals.

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?
Divide the minutes by 60. For example 40 minutes ÷ 60 = 0.67 hours.

Does the break count as work time?
Short breaks (usually under 20 minutes) are generally paid; meal breaks of 30 minutes or more are often unpaid. Check your state rules.
