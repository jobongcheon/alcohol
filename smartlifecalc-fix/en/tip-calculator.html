How much to tip — and how to split it fairly

Tipping customs in the United States are generous by world standards, because many service workers depend on tips for most of their income. This calculator works out the tip, the total and each person's share, and lets you round up so nobody has to deal with cents.

How to use the tip calculator

  1. Enter the bill amount.
  2. Choose a tip percentage or enter your own.
  3. Enter how many people are splitting the bill.
  4. Press Calculate Tip.

The formula

Tip = bill × tip %  |  each person = (bill + tip) ÷ people

Worked example

A dinner bill of $86.00 with an 18% tip: the tip is $15.48 and the total is $101.48. Split four ways, that's $25.37 each. Rounded up to $26 each, the table pays $104, which is a tip of about 21%.

Why the law lets restaurants pay servers $2.13 an hour

U.S. tipping feels mandatory because, for many servers, it is most of their pay. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act an employer may pay a tipped employee a cash wage of $2.13 an hour and count up to $5.12 in tips toward the $7.25 federal minimum wage. If tips fall short in a pay period, the employer must make up the difference, but the baseline the law guarantees is still $7.25.

States differ. Many set a higher cash wage for tipped workers, and several, including California, Oregon and Washington, do not allow a tip credit at all, so servers there earn the full state minimum before tips. Your 20% means something different in each place, but it is still customary everywhere.

One more legal wrinkle: a mandatory service charge printed on the bill is not a tip under federal rules. It belongs to the business unless the business chooses to pass it on. If you want to be sure the server gets something, ask whether the charge is shared, or leave a small extra amount in cash.

Before or after tax?

Etiquette guides usually say to tip on the pre-tax amount, though many people tip on the total for simplicity. On an $86 bill with 8% tax, the difference at 18% is only about $1.24.

Splitting when people ordered differently

An even split is simplest for friends. If one person had a much larger order, add up each person's items, apply the same tip percentage to each, and divide tax proportionally. Check the receipt for an automatic service charge — many restaurants add 18% for large groups, so you don't tip twice.

Common tipping questions

Do I tip on a discounted meal?
It's considered polite to tip on the original price before the discount or coupon.

What about tipping abroad?
Customs vary widely: in Japan tipping is generally not expected, while in much of Europe a small round-up is common. Check local custom before you go.

Should I tip on the total or before tax?
Either is acceptable; tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is the traditional rule.

How much should I tip for takeout?
Tipping is optional for takeout; 10% is a kind gesture for large or complicated orders.
