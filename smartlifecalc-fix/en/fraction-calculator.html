Working with fractions, step by step

Fractions trip people up because each operation has its own rule. The calculator does the arithmetic and simplifies the answer, but it helps to see why the result is right — especially when you are checking homework or cutting wood.

How to use the fraction calculator

  1. Type the top and bottom numbers of each fraction.
  2. Choose +, −, × or ÷.
  3. Press Calculate.

The formula

a/b + c/d = (ad + bc) ÷ bd  |  a/b × c/d = ac ÷ bd  |  a/b ÷ c/d = ad ÷ bc

Four worked examples

  • Add: 1/2 + 1/3. The common denominator is 6, so 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
  • Subtract: 5/6 − 1/4. Use 12 as the denominator: 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12.
  • Multiply: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15, which simplifies to 2/5.
  • Divide: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6. Six one-eighth pieces fit into three quarters.

Sixteenths on a U.S. tape measure

A U.S. tape measure splits each inch into 16 parts. The longest marks are inches, then halves, quarters, eighths and finally the short sixteenth marks. To read a length, count the small marks past the last whole inch: five marks past 7 is 7 5/16 in.

Adding two measurements means putting both in sixteenths: 7 5/16 + 3 7/8 = 117/16 + 62/16 = 179/16 = 11 3/16 in. Then remember the saw blade. A typical blade removes about 1/8 in, so cutting a 48 in board into two equal pieces gives 23 15/16 in each, not 24.

Mixed numbers in real life

The calculator takes simple fractions, so turn a mixed number into an improper fraction first: multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator. 2 1/2 becomes 5/2 and 1 3/4 becomes 7/4. Then 5/2 + 7/4 = 10/4 + 7/4 = 17/4, shown as the mixed number 4 1/4.

Woodworking: a board is 5 3/8 inches and you cut off 2 7/16 inches. Enter 43/8 − 39/16. The answer is 47/16, or 2 15/16 inches left.

Recipes: making a recipe 1.5 times bigger? Multiply 3/4 cup by 3/2 to get 9/8 — that is 1 1/8 cups.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the bottoms: 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Denominators only stay the same, they are never added.
  • Forgetting to flip when dividing: dividing by 1/8 is the same as multiplying by 8.
  • Losing the minus sign: put a negative sign on the top number only, such as −3/4.
  • Not simplifying: 6/15 and 2/5 are equal, but most teachers expect the simplest form.

Two things to double-check

  • Always simplify by dividing top and bottom by their greatest common factor.

Five ways to tell which fraction is bigger

Which is larger, 5/8 or 11/16? Work down this list and stop at the first rule that applies.

  1. Same denominator: compare the tops. 7/12 is bigger than 5/12.
  2. Same numerator: the smaller bottom wins, because its pieces are larger. 3/5 is bigger than 3/8.
  3. Benchmark against one half: 7/15 is under a half (half of 15 is 7.5) and 9/17 is over it (half of 17 is 8.5), so 9/17 is bigger.
  4. Cross-multiply: for a/b against c/d, compare a × d with c × b. For 5/8 and 11/16: 5 × 16 = 80 and 11 × 8 = 88, so 11/16 is bigger.
  5. Convert to decimals when the denominators are awkward: 7/12 = 0.583 and 4/7 = 0.571, so 7/12 wins by about 0.012.

Rule 4 works every time; rules 1 to 3 are faster in your head and a good check on any answer the tool gives you.

Fraction questions students and DIYers ask

Why does the calculator show a decimal too?
Decimals are easier to compare. 5/6 = 0.833333, which is a little more than 4/5 = 0.8.

Can I use the common denominator a × b every time?
Yes, multiplying the two denominators always works, but the answer then needs more simplifying. The least common denominator keeps the numbers small.

What is an improper fraction?
A fraction whose top is larger than its bottom, such as 17/4. It equals the mixed number 4 1/4.

How do I simplify a fraction?
Divide the top and bottom by their greatest common factor. 12/16 ÷ 4 = 3/4.

How do I enter a mixed number?
Convert it first: 2 1/2 = (2 × 2 + 1)/2 = 5/2.
