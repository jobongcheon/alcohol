Body Mass Index (BMI) is a quick screening number that compares your weight with your height. Enter your measurements in feet and pounds or centimeters and kilograms to see your BMI and healthy weight range.

What your BMI does — and doesn't — tell you

Body mass index compares weight with height. It is a quick screening number doctors use to spot possible weight-related health risks across large groups of people. It does not measure body fat directly, so it should be read alongside other information such as waist size, blood pressure and blood tests.

How to use the bmi calculator

The formula

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²  =  weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Worked example

Height 5 ft 9 in (69 inches), weight 170 lb. BMI = 703 × 170 ÷ 69² = 25.1, just inside the "overweight" range. In metric: 77 kg ÷ (1.75 m)² ≈ 25.1.

Pounds per BMI point, by height

Because BMI divides weight by height squared, one BMI point is a different number of pounds depending on how tall you are. The conversion is height in inches squared ÷ 703.

Worked case: someone 5 ft 4 in and 160 lb has a BMI of 703 × 160 ÷ 4,096 = 27.5. Reaching 24.9 means dropping about 2.6 points × 5.8 lb, roughly 15 lb, to about 145 lb. In metric, one BMI point equals your height in meters squared, in kilograms: at 1.70 m, 2.89 kg.

Limits of BMI

  • Muscle – athletes can have a high BMI with little body fat.
  • Age – older adults may have more body fat at the same BMI.
  • Ethnicity – some health bodies use lower cut-offs for people of Asian descent.
  • Children and teens – BMI is interpreted with age- and sex-specific percentile charts, not the adult table.
  • Pregnancy – BMI is not used during pregnancy.

A useful companion: waist size

Fat around the abdomen is linked to higher risk. Many guidelines treat a waist over about 40 inches (102 cm) for men or 35 inches (88 cm) for women as a sign to talk with a doctor, even when BMI looks normal.

Worth knowing

  • Muscular people may have a high BMI without extra fat.
  • Children and teens use BMI-for-age percentiles instead.

Three cases where the standard cut-offs can mislead

  1. Asian ancestry. A World Health Organization expert group suggested lower action points for Asian populations, 23 for overweight and 27.5 for obesity, because health risks appear at lower BMIs. The American Diabetes Association recommends diabetes screening for Asian Americans from a BMI of 23 rather than 25.
  2. Height that has changed. Many adults lose height with age but keep the number from an old driver's license. At 170 lb, a height of 69 in gives a BMI of 25.1; the same weight at 68 in gives 25.8. Measure yourself before you enter the number.
  3. Muscle and frame. Someone who lifts weights can read 27 with a 32-inch waist and low body fat. In that case the waist measurement and a blood pressure and cholesterol check say more than the table. The NIH describes BMI as a screening step, not a measure of body fat.

None of these changes the calculation, only what the number means; if one applies to you, treat the result as a prompt to talk with a clinician.

What people want to know about BMI

How much should I lose to change category?
Use the healthy-weight table on this page for your height; small changes of 5–10% of body weight already improve many health markers.

Is BMI a diagnosis?
No. It's a screening tool. Talk with a health professional about your own situation.

Is BMI accurate?
It is a useful screening tool for groups, but it does not separate muscle from fat. Use it alongside waist size and a doctor’s advice.

What BMI is considered obese?
For adults, a BMI of 30 or higher is classified as obesity by the CDC and WHO.
