Dice odds made simple

Every die roll here is independent and uses your browser’s cryptographic random number generator, so no number is favored. Knowing the odds helps with board games and tabletop RPGs.

How to use the dice roller

  1. Choose how many dice and how many sides.
  2. Add a modifier if your game uses one.
  3. Press Roll the Dice as many times as you like.

Odds of rolling at least one six

Many games only need a six (or a natural 20) on at least one die. The quickest way to find that chance is backwards: work out the odds that every die misses, then subtract from 100%. One die misses a six 5 times in 6, so with several dice you multiply 5/6 by itself once per die.

d20 rolls

  • Each face of a d20 has a 5% chance, so a natural 20 comes up 1 time in 20.
  • Rolling two d20s and keeping the higher one (“advantage”) gives a 20 about 9.75% of the time.
  • A modifier just shifts the total: 1d20+5 ranges from 6 to 25.

Common mistakes

  • The gambler’s fallacy: after three 1s in a row, a 1 is still exactly as likely next roll.
  • Thinking all totals are equal: with two or more dice, middle totals are much more common.

Fair rolls for games and classrooms

Board games, role-playing games and classroom probability lessons all need fair dice. An online roller is useful when dice are missing, when you need many dice at once, or when a game uses unusual dice such as a d12 or d100.

Teachers can use it to run quick experiments: roll 2d6 fifty times as a class, tally the totals, and compare the results with the probability table above. With more rolls, the results get closer to the expected pattern.

Dice myths versus the math

  • Myth: 3d6 and 1d18+2 are the same because both run from 3 to 18. Fact: With three dice, 10 and 11 each come up 12.5% of the time while 3 or 18 appear once in 216 rolls. A single d18 would give every total the same 5.6%.
  • Myth: Rolling with disadvantage (two d20, keep the lower) halves your chance of a 20. Fact: Both dice must show 20, so the chance falls from 5% to 0.25%, one roll in 400.
  • Myth: A +5 modifier makes high results more likely. Fact: It shifts every outcome up by 5; each of the 20 totals from 6 to 25 still has a 5% chance.
  • Myth: Three sixes in a row prove a die is loaded. Fact: On a fair d6 that streak is 1 in 216, so it will turn up a few times over a long campaign.
  • Myth: On percentile dice, 00 on the tens die and 0 on the ones die means zero. Fact: The usual convention reads that pair as 100.

Dice questions from game night

Can I use this for real-money gambling?
No. It is meant for games and teaching probability.

Why keep the last five rolls?
So everyone at the table can check what was rolled.

Is the online dice roller really random?
Yes. It uses the browser’s cryptographic random generator, so no number is favored.

What is a d20?
A 20-sided die, used for most checks in games like Dungeons & Dragons.
