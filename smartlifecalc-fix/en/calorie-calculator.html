How many calories do you burn in a day? This calculator estimates your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), then shows calorie targets for losing or gaining weight.

Understanding BMR, TDEE and your daily calories

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the energy your body uses at complete rest — breathing, circulation, keeping warm. TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) adds everything you do: walking, work, exercise. This calculator estimates BMR with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, which studies have found to be one of the more accurate formulas for most adults, then multiplies it by an activity factor.

How to use the calorie calculator

  1. Choose your sex and enter your age.
  2. Enter your height and weight in US or metric units.
  3. Pick your activity level.
  4. Press Calculate Calories.

The formula

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women)  |  TDEE = BMR × activity

Worked example

A 40-year-old man, 178 cm (5 ft 10 in), 80 kg (176 lb): BMR = 10 × 80 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 40 + 5 ≈ 1,718 kcal. With moderate exercise 3–5 days a week (×1.55), TDEE ≈ 2,660 kcal a day.

Myth versus fact: calorie claims that do not survive the math

  • Myth: a 500-calorie daily deficit always loses 52 pounds a year. Fact: in the Mifflin-St Jeor equation every kilogram lost lowers BMR by about 10 calories a day, so the same menu buys a smaller deficit as weight falls and loss slows.
  • Myth: food eaten after 8 p.m. turns to fat. Fact: the daily balance of calories in and out drives weight; timing matters mostly through sleep and appetite.
  • Myth: a pound of muscle burns 50 calories a day. Fact: resting muscle uses roughly 6 calories per pound per day, about three times fat tissue.
  • Myth: 2,000 calories is the right number for everyone. Fact: 2,000 is the reference used for percent Daily Values on Nutrition Facts labels. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans estimate adult needs at roughly 1,600–2,400 calories for women and 2,000–3,000 for men, depending on age and activity.
  • Myth: drinks are a rounding error. Fact: alcohol has about 7 calories per gram, so a 5-ounce glass of wine is around 120 calories before any mixer.

Using the number for a goal

  • Maintain: eat close to your TDEE.
  • Lose weight gradually: a deficit of about 300–500 kcal a day is a common, sustainable starting point.
  • Gain muscle: a small surplus (about 200–300 kcal) with strength training.

Most people overestimate their activity level. If your weight doesn't change as expected after 2–3 weeks, adjust by 100–200 kcal rather than making big cuts.

Safety notes

Very low intakes are hard to sustain and can cause nutrient shortfalls. People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, or managing a medical condition should get personal advice from a doctor or registered dietitian.

Small details that change the result

  • Protein and fiber help you feel full on fewer calories.
  • Recalculate after every 10 lb of weight change.

Worked case: why the same menu stops working after 15 pounds

A 35-year-old woman, 165 cm (5 ft 5 in) and 75 kg (165 lb), with light activity (multiplier 1.375):

  • BMR = 10 × 75 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 35 − 161 = 1,445 calories.
  • TDEE = 1,445 × 1.375 ≈ 1,987 calories. A 400-calorie deficit means eating about 1,590.
  • After losing 7 kg (about 15 lb) to 68 kg: BMR = 1,375 and TDEE ≈ 1,891.
  • Still eating 1,590, the deficit is now about 300 calories, roughly a quarter smaller, so weekly loss slows in step.

The fix is to recalculate at each 10-pound milestone and either trim 100 calories or add activity, not to cut sharply, while staying above the usual floors of about 1,200 calories for women and 1,500 for men without medical supervision. Calorie counting questions, answered

Why is my BMR lower than I expected?
BMR drops with age and with lower muscle mass. Strength training helps maintain it.

Do I eat back exercise calories?
If you chose an activity level that already includes exercise, no — it's counted in your TDEE.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?
BMR is what your body burns at complete rest. TDEE adds daily movement and exercise.

Is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation accurate?
It is considered one of the most accurate simple formulas, usually within about 10% for most people.
