Decimals and fractions: when each one is easier

Measurements, recipes and school math switch between decimals and fractions all the time. Decimals are easier to compare and type into a calculator; fractions match tape measures, wrenches and measuring cups. This converter goes both ways and always simplifies.

How to use the decimal to fraction calculator

  1. Choose the direction.
  2. Enter the decimal or the fraction.
  3. Press Convert.

The formula

Decimal → fraction: write it over 10, 100, 1000… then simplify (0.625 = 625/1000 = 5/8)

Worked examples

  • 0.625 = 625/1000. Divide top and bottom by 125 → 5/8.
  • 1.75 = 175/100 = 7/4 = 1 3/4.
  • 0.4 = 4/10 = 2/5.
  • 3/16 = 3 ÷ 16 = 0.1875, or 18.75%.

Reading a tape measure

Most tape measures in the U.S. are marked in sixteenths of an inch. To turn a decimal into the nearest mark, multiply the decimal part by 16 and round. For 2.3 inches: 0.3 × 16 = 4.8, which rounds to 5, so the mark is 2 5/16 inches. For metric drawings, remember 1 inch = 25.4 mm.

Repeating decimals

Some fractions never end as decimals: 1/3 = 0.333… and 1/6 = 0.1666…. If you type 0.333, the converter treats it as exactly 333/1000, because that is what you entered. When you know the value is a repeating decimal, enter the fraction (1/3) instead — that keeps your answer exact.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the wrong number of places: 0.05 is 5/100 (= 1/20), not 5/10.
  • Stopping before simplifying: 75/100 should be written as 3/4.
  • Mixing up percent and decimal: 0.5% is 0.005, not 0.5.

When converting helps most

Woodworkers and DIYers convert decimal measurements from plans or digital calipers into the nearest 1/16 or 1/32 inch on a tape. Cooks turn recipe scaling results such as 0.75 cup into 3/4 cup. Students check homework answers that must be written as simplified fractions.

A good habit is to keep full precision while you calculate and convert to a fraction only at the end. Rounding to the nearest 1/16 inch at every step can add up to a noticeable error over several cuts.

Why your timesheet says 7.75 hours, not 7:45

Payroll software stores time as decimal hours because decimals multiply cleanly by a wage. The conversion is minutes ÷ 60: 15 minutes is 0.25 hour, 20 minutes is 0.33, 45 minutes is 0.75, and 6 minutes is 0.1.

A week of 41 hours 20 minutes is 41.33 hours. For a non-exempt worker earning $18.50 an hour, that is 40 × $18.50 = $740.00 plus 1.33 overtime hours at $27.75 = $37.00, for $777.00. Type 41.20 instead of 41.33 and the check comes out $3.70 short, a small error that repeats every week.

Federal rules allow employers to round clock punches to the nearest quarter hour, as long as the rounding does not consistently favor the employer. Converting your own hours to decimals and checking them against the stub is the simplest way to catch a system that rounds the wrong way.

Decimal and fraction questions from the workshop and the classroom

How do I convert a fraction to a percent?
Divide the top by the bottom and multiply by 100. 7/8 = 0.875 = 87.5%.

What is the decimal for 1/16 inch?
0.0625 inches, which is about 1.59 mm.

Why is 0.1 + 0.2 not exactly 0.3 on some computers?
Computers store decimals in binary, which cannot represent 0.1 exactly. Fractions avoid that problem; this converter works with whole numbers internally to stay exact.

How do I convert 0.4 to a fraction?
0.4 = 4/10 = 2/5.

How do I turn a fraction into a percent?
Divide top by bottom and multiply by 100: 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%.
